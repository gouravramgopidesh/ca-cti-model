Phishing uses deceptive messages to persuade recipients to disclose information or perform unsafe actions. Common techniques include impersonating trusted organizations, creating urgency and linking to fraudulent login pages. Spear phishing targets a specific individual or organization using tailored details. Analysts examine sender identity, message headers, links and attachment metadata. A suspicious message should be reported through approved channels rather than forwarded broadly. Multifactor authentication, email filtering and user training can reduce risk. Defenders can use domain registration information and authentication results as supporting context. No single indicator proves malicious intent, and investigation should consider the complete evidence.
Credential theft can lead to unauthorized account access even after a phishing message is removed. Incident responders should review login activity, revoke suspicious sessions and reset affected credentials according to policy. Phishing-resistant authentication can reduce dependence on passwords and one-time codes. Organizations should monitor lookalike domains and provide clear reporting instructions. Analysts should avoid opening suspicious attachments outside an isolated analysis environment. An investigation report should document observed indicators, affected users, confidence and recommended mitigations. These records support future detection and allow teams to identify repeated campaigns.

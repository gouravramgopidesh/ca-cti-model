Threat actor intelligence describes adversaries, their objectives, capabilities and observed behaviors. Attribution is often uncertain because different groups may use similar tools or deliberately imitate one another. Analysts should distinguish evidence from assumptions and describe confidence explicitly. The MITRE ATT&CK knowledge base organizes observed adversary tactics and techniques. Mapping incidents to techniques can help defenders identify gaps in monitoring and plan exercises. Threat intelligence may include campaign timelines, malware families and infrastructure relationships. Graph databases are useful for exploring connections among entities, but a graph relationship is not by itself proof of attribution or malicious intent.
Operational intelligence supports decisions about detection, containment and recovery. Strategic intelligence provides broader context about trends and risks. Tactical intelligence describes techniques and observable artifacts that defenders can use in investigations. A useful report connects findings to the organization's assets and security priorities. Analysts should cite sources and track disagreements among reports. Information sharing with trusted partners can improve awareness while protecting sensitive details. Threat models should be updated as adversary behavior changes. Effective intelligence programs measure whether findings help teams improve defenses and reduce response time.

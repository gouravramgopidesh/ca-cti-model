Ransomware is malicious software that denies access to systems or data, commonly by encrypting files. Attackers may also steal information and threaten to publish it. Threat intelligence analysts study reported incidents, indicators of compromise and attacker behavior to assess exposure. Initial access can involve phishing, compromised credentials or exploitation of vulnerable services. Defenders should maintain offline or immutable backups and regularly test restoration. Network segmentation can restrict lateral movement. Multifactor authentication and timely patching reduce common entry opportunities. Endpoint monitoring can identify unusual file modifications, suspicious processes and attempts to disable security software. Incident response teams should isolate affected devices and preserve evidence.
A ransomware investigation should distinguish confirmed observations from hypotheses. Analysts can compare file hashes, domains and network addresses against trusted reports, but shared infrastructure can create false positives. Recovery should prioritize critical services and verify restored systems before reconnecting them. Backups must be protected from the same credentials used to administer production systems. An incident timeline helps responders understand initial access, execution, persistence and data movement. Organizations should document lessons learned and update controls after containment. Threat intelligence is useful when it leads to specific defensive decisions rather than simply accumulating indicators.

Vulnerability management identifies, prioritizes and remediates weaknesses in software and infrastructure. CVE identifiers provide a common reference for publicly disclosed vulnerabilities. Severity scores help describe technical impact, but organizations also need to consider asset exposure, exploitation evidence and business criticality. A vulnerability that is actively exploited may require urgent action even when other weaknesses have higher numerical severity. Threat intelligence provides context about exploitation campaigns and affected products. Defenders should maintain an accurate inventory, apply vendor updates and validate remediation. When immediate patching is impractical, compensating controls may reduce exposure until a permanent fix is available.
Security teams should distinguish a published vulnerability from a confirmed compromise. Scanning can identify potentially affected systems, while logs and endpoint telemetry may reveal exploitation attempts. Prioritization should be revisited as new evidence arrives. Change management and testing help prevent operational disruption during patch deployment. Asset owners should receive clear remediation deadlines and instructions. Metrics such as time to remediate and coverage of critical assets can help measure progress. Documentation should retain sources, dates and the reasoning behind decisions so that another analyst can reproduce the assessment.

Indicators of compromise are observable artifacts associated with possible malicious activity. Examples include file hashes, domains, IP addresses and suspicious registry entries. Analysts collect indicators from incident reports, network telemetry and security tools. Indicators have different lifetimes and reliability. A file hash identifies specific content, whereas an IP address may be reused by unrelated services. Context such as source, observation date, confidence and associated behavior is essential. Threat intelligence platforms should normalize indicator formats and preserve provenance. Defenders can enrich indicators with observations from independent sources and use them to prioritize investigation rather than automatically assume every match is malicious.
Indicator sharing helps teams recognize related incidents, but sensitive information must be handled according to applicable policies. STIX represents structured cyber threat intelligence and TAXII supports its exchange. Analysts should record when an indicator was first and last observed and review stale entries. Confidence scores express uncertainty rather than certainty. Detection rules may combine indicators with behavioral signals to improve precision. A threat intelligence report should separate raw observations from analyst assessments and give readers sufficient context to make an informed decision. Regular feedback from incident responders improves the usefulness of the intelligence collection.
